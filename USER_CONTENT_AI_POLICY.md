# Generative AI disclosure policy
Any publicly listed content on any official Resonite channels produced with clear use of generative AI tools must be clearly labeled by adding the `AI_GeneratedContent` component in the hierarchy and saved with the content.

We do not require more than one part of the hierarchy to be tagged for published content. More granular tagging is recommended for better filtering, but not required.

## Why is this required?
Resonite is a diverse platform with differing cultures, opinions and values. We want those to be able to coexist, regardless of our differences and personal opinions. We as a platform also value freedom of exploration, self determination and giving each user ability to curate their own Resonite experience.

Large group of our community as well as the outside world has a strong negative stance towards generative AI, due to variety of moral, ethical, safety, economical and other concerns.

This tagging policy was devised to give users a choice on whether to engage with content produced using this technology, without restricting other users from using this technology, preserving freedoms of both sides.

## When do I need to tag my content?
1) When you have used a generative AI functionality directly to produce the content yourself
2) When you sourced the content from a 3rd party which included clear AI labeling
3) You incorporated generative AI features into your content (based on [Article 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/transparency-rules-article-50/) of the EU AI Act):
    1. when AI interacts directly with people
    2. when AI generates synthetic content
    3. when AI is used for emotion recognition or biometric categorization
    4. when AI creates deepfakes or text published on matters of public interest

## What if I'm unsure about the AI status?
We do not require performing extensive research to determine AI status of the content if the labeling is not readily apparent.

Examples include:
- Tool uses generative AI internally, but does not make this clear to its users
- 3rd party source for the asset is missing clear labeling or is mislabeled

In such situations you are free to publish the content without the labeling.

However if it comes to light the content did use generative AI, we do expect you to correct the situation (see below for more).

## What is publicly listed content on official Resonite channels?
Any content that is submited to be listed on official Resonite channels & content feeds is covered by this policy. Publishing the same content through other channels does not require labeling. A channel/feed is considered to be official if it would be accessible and visible to any user with default account configuration.

Examples include:
- Worlds submitted to the "Worlds" tab of the default Resonite group
- Future Workshop (will be expanded once workshop is introduced)
- Public Folders submitted to be shared with the community in Resonite Essentials
    - Take extra care when saving new items into these folders once they're shared
- Items & Worlds submitted to Resonite team for promotion (e.g. on streams)

### What is NOT publicly listed content?
If you publish your content solely outside of the official channels, you do not need to follow the policy. However tagging is still encouraged, because if the content gets at any point published though the official channels, it will need to follow the policy.

Examples include:
- Exporting content as ResonitePackage and publishing externally
- Public Folders shared with friends or own groups
    - Resonite does not have official public folder listing & search functionality - finding a public folder requires explicit actions from users to save them into their inventory
- Sending items to contacts via messages
- Worlds submitted to other groups than the official Resonite group
    - While not currently a commonly used functionality, it allows for creating your own feeds for submitted content
    - Each group sets its own rules for its feed
- Private use
    - If you never publish the content with wider community and only use it yourself or with your friends, no labeling is necessary

## What type of content does need to be labeled?
Any content that features any assets or data produced or modified with the use of generative AI functionality need to be labeled. This includes:
- Text
- Textures
- Models
- Audio
- Video
- Code (ProtoFlux or other)
- Objects / Worlds
- Content that interfaces with generative AI systems
- Other binary data

### What is an use of generative AI tool?
- Generating content directly
- Modifying existing non-AI content with generative AI functionality
- Generating content which is then manipulated and modified manually
- Generating sketches, ideas and other resources based on which the content is made

In short, if generative AI functionality has been used to inspire, modify or completely create any aspects of the published content and you are aware of it, we require the content to be tagged.

Using a pre-existing tools that have been made or modified with generative AI is **NOT** considered use of generative AI as long as you don't use any generative AI functionality.

We strongly encourage to use granular tagging based on your use, as it will allow for more fine-grained filtering for other users and better communicate the use. However a simple presence of `AI_GeneratedContent` is sufficient to comply with this policy.

When using fine grained tagging, the categories must remain accurate to the actual use.

# Human made tag
Content creators who can guarantee that their content has been produced or sourced without use of generative AI can optionally mark it with `<INSERT_COMPONENT_NAME_HERE>` to indicate this status.

This tagging is optional, but subject to the same policy. It can be applied if and only if all following conditions apply:
- The content would not be required to have AI tag based on any rules above
- The AI status of any of the content is NOT unknown - in other words, the user needs to be sure it was NOT made with AI

This tag presents much stronger determination of the status and the user is expected to perform more extensive research into the tools and/or 3rd party sources to ensure the fully human made status.

# What if I find published content that's improperly tagged?
If you come across content that is improperly tagged, please send a ticket to moderation.resonite.com.

**DO NOT** contact or harass the user directly.

Your ticket must contain clear evidence showing that the contact is AI generated. The burden of proof is on the reporter, moderation will not investigate AI use. Tickets that are incomplete or contain on actionable evidence will be closed without a response.

## What is clear evidence?
Examples of clear evidence include:
- Markers in the asset data (or metadata) indicating use of generative AI functionality
- Link to a 3rd party website with the identical asset clearly labeled as produced with generative AI
- Posts on social media and other public spaces of the user stating they used generative AI to produce reported content

## What is NOT clear evidence?
We will not action any tickets where it is not clear that given content is produced with generative AI. Examples include:
- Claims of "it looks AI generated"
- AI detection tools without a solid track record of avoiding false positives
- User using generative AI to produce other content that is not part of the report

We understand that this allows AI generated content to slip through and remain untagged. Unfortunately if we cannot make a clear determination of AI use, we cannot enforce the tagging. We are also risking putting creators who do not use generative AI into a position of having to defending their work as not being AI produced, which we want to avoid.

## Correcting AI tagging
If we find a ticket contains credible evidence of AI use in an improperly labeled content, the user may receive a message from the Resonite account in the contacts, detailing instructions how to correct the tagging. This message will include the evidence provided in the ticket, but will not identify the reporter. The message will alert the user that they will have a grace period of 14 days to update their world before a general "catch all" tag is added automatically.

User can resolve the situation in following ways:
1) Apply proper tagging based on the evidence and re-save
2) Remove assets with AI use and re-save
3) Create a ticket contesting the provided evidence

Resolving the situation automatically cancels 14 day period for automated tagging.

The "catch all" tag is not granular (meaning it will be filtered when any AI aspects are filtered). The tag will also be preserved when the content is re-saved without any `AI_GeneratedContent` components present and must be removed manually from the tag list.

In a case of 14 day period passing with no action from the user, the user is free to free to manually remove the "catch all" AI tag provided they correct the tagging. This de-listing does NOT put any negative marks or restrictions on the account.

## Grandfathered content
Any published content that was last updated before this policy took place does not require to be tagged to comply. Any changes and additions to this content are covered by this policy and must be tagged appropriately. 

Examples:
- You never update your grandfathered content:
    - No action required from your end
    - We WILL NOT accept ticket reports of mistagged content
- You update your grandfathered content without use of AI:
    - No tagging necessary
    - You are **NOT** required to audit the existing unchanged content for AI usage
    - However we WILL accept ticket reports of mistagged content, even of existing parts
- You update your grandfathered content with the use of AI:
     - Tagging is required for the additions/changes as per this policy
     - You are **NOT** required to audit the existing unchanged content for AI usage
     - However we WILL accept ticket reports of mistagged content, even of existing parts

## Bad faith actors
If we believe a user is engaging with the tagging system in a bad faith, such as:
- Re-saving the world without correcting the tagging to cancel 14 day grace period
- Removing the "catch all" AI tag manually without correcting the tagging
- Removing the tags at later point without removing the affected content
- Repeatedly marking content with "Human made tag" despite evidence of use of generative AI functionality 
- Generally making no effort to use the system in a good faith, e.g. by repeatedly publishing large amounts of clearly mistagged content

We may send the user a warning, detailing this behavior. If user continues this behavior despite the warning, we may temporarily restrict ability to publish content in public world list (and upcoming Workshop) for 30 days, escalating with further incidents. Appeals can be made at moderation.resonite.com
